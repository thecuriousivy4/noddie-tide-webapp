**Noddie** — a heritage register where no dig is permitted unless the elders holding that Country agree. **No nod, no dig.**

**[Live demo](https://noddie.onrender.com/)** · `joan` / `noddie-joan`

### 1. Vision & originality

In May 2020 Rio Tinto legally destroyed 46,000-year-old rock shelters at Juukan Gorge, using a ministerial consent granted seven years earlier, before excavations revealed what was there.

Registers fail twice: consent goes stale, and protection demands disclosure. Noddie fixes both: consent is a quorum that must hold *now*, and the register stores what it cannot read.

### 2. Trust constraint insight

Communities must surrender the secret to protect it: name, boundary and significance go into a database any officer with a login can read, and the final say sits with a minister. Many decline, so bulldozers find what nobody recorded.

### 3. Value & impact

An elder records a women's-restricted site she would never hand a government database; not even a fellow custodian can open it. Communities can record sites they now keep secret, proponents learn a site exists before plans are drawn, not mid-dig, and governments get a fuller register without holding the secrets.

### 4. Why TideCloak matters

Site details are encrypted in the elder's browser under a TideCloak policy; our server stores only ciphertext. Permit authority lives in a token signed by Tide's decentralised network, not a database column. Each Traditional Owner corporation sets its own quorum, and no administrator or minister can change it or issue a permit alone. With ordinary login, our admin could.

### 5. Prototype & storytelling

**Key moment:** the Minister presses **Record consent** and the tally doesn't move.

1. An elder records a site; the server sees only ciphertext.
2. A proponent searches and sees only: *something is recorded here, call this number*.
3. Two elders approve; the permit commits, but the proponent stays blocked until their token is reissued.
4. Revocation runs in reverse: no one withdraws a permit alone.
